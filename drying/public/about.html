About Watch the Drying

I'm Robin, and I started this small browser game with two deliberately ordinary questions: what does watching paint dry sound like, and what sound would an eye make when it blinks? I turned painting a wall and waiting for it to dry into a gentle challenge with a surprising amount to pay attention to.

Two parts to each round

First, choose a paint colour and cover the marked wall before the painting timer runs out. Completing the wall earns a bonus. Then the pace changes: during the drying phase, stay with the wall and watch the animated eyes to collect Moments of Calm. Keep your finger or pointer pressed down and steer the eyes across painted wall; moving off the paint or lifting away interrupts the watching.

Moments of Calm are the game’s score and upgrade currency. Between rounds, spend them on upgrades that change how efficiently you paint or how much calm you can collect. The challenge is to earn enough through careful painting and focused watching to make the next round easier.

A game about paying attention

Most timed games reward speed from beginning to end. Watch the Drying deliberately changes gears: it asks for a quick, complete painting pass, then rewards patience and steady attention. The eyes give the waiting phase a small task, so it feels active without turning it into a race.

I added sound after the central play idea was working. The result is a set of playful cues for a scene that normally has no soundtrack at all. I wrote a short note, Noisy Drying, about the questions that inspired those sounds.

Made for a browser and a finger

The game runs in a web browser and supports touch as well as a mouse. Its small targets and pointer-following eyes are arranged so your finger does not cover the things you need to watch. Later refinements also focused on reducing unnecessary redraw work on mobile screens, keeping the same idea comfortable to play on a phone.

This is one of several projects Dad and I have grown from odd questions through play, sound and small practical improvements. I tell the story behind the wider collection on About Andisdad Games.
