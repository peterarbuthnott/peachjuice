About Andisdad Games

I'm Robin, and Andisdad is the collection of browser games and experiments I make with my dad, Peter. We start with ideas that are too interesting to leave in a notebook: sometimes we revive an old one, sometimes we shape a new one, and sometimes we turn scraps of PowerPoint notes into something people can play.

Some of our ideas had been waiting through work, family life and the usual lack of spare time. In 2026, coding assistants helped us get from a conversation to a working version quickly, leaving more time for the second pass: fixing awkward bits, improving the mobile experience, adding sound and making each project feel more finished. The ideas and decisions are ours; the tools help us build and refine them.

What you can play

  • Dullas Dishwater turns washing up into a timed arcade loop of scrubbing, stacking clean plates and choosing upgrades.
  • Watch the Drying pairs a quick wall-painting challenge with a slower phase of watching animated eyes and collecting Moments of Calm.
  • Nominate helps a group keep bids, tricks and cumulative scores during a game of Nomination Whist.
  • The Standup Memory Game turns a prepared set of meeting topics into a short quiz with answers, feedback and a score board.
  • Britain in six strands lets you explore British history through six timelines that you can compare and browse together.

Make the ordinary a little stranger

Two of our games started with deliberately odd questions: what would washing dishes look like as an arcade game, and what sound would drying paint or a blinking eye make? We weren't trying to make the chores realistic. We wanted to find a playful rhythm in small actions—scrub, watch, collect, upgrade—and give them personality through movement and sound.

Nominate takes a more practical route. I made it to help a group keep a real card game moving, with the round sequence and scoring visible so everyone can concentrate on playing. Its rules page explains the scoring this version uses and the house-rule choices we may add later. The Standup Memory Game is another small experiment: a quick way to test what details from a prepared set of meeting questions stayed in mind.

Built, played, improved

The first playable version is a beginning, not a finish line. Dad and I try each idea, notice where it drags, and work through the small improvements that are easy to postpone: responsive controls, fewer unnecessary redraws on phones, lighter files, clearer feedback and sound that fits the action. A feature can be simple and still benefit from careful design.

These are independent web projects. I give each game its own page with instructions and details about how it works. The collection continues to grow as new ideas become playable.
