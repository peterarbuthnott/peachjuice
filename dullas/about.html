About Dullas Dishwater

I'm Robin, and I made Dullas Dishwater by turning a familiar chore into a small arcade game. Move a sponge over dirty plates, clean them before time runs out, and use the plates you earn to improve your next round.

How a run works

Each round gives you a stack of plates to wash. Move the sponge across a plate until enough of its surface is clean, then continue to the next one. The timer adds pressure, while finishing quickly can earn a speed bonus. Cleaned plates become the resource you spend on upgrades.

Upgrades let you shape the run. Some make scrubbing more effective, some increase the sponge’s reach, and others improve the rewards you collect. A larger pile arrives as the rounds progress, so the useful choice is the one that suits your current bottleneck: cleaning faster, reaching more of the plate, or getting more value from each completed dish.

Clean plates also go into a drying stack. Filling the stack gives an extra reward, which makes the end of a round part of the strategy too. A run can continue for up to 35 rounds, or finish earlier if every upgrade reaches its limit. The game keeps a run so you can return to it later.

From a mundane job to a game

My starting question was simple: could washing up be made playful? I built the answer around movement, visible progress, short deadlines and upgrades. It is intentionally a little silly; the satisfaction comes from turning a pile of dirty plates into a clean, growing result.

I added sound after the main game loop worked. The cues are small browser-generated tones and soft bubbly scrubbing sounds, adding texture without needing recorded sound files. That sequence—make the game work, then add sound and polish—is part of how our games have grown.

Playing on different screens

Dullas runs in a browser and supports mouse and touch play. The controls are built around moving the sponge rather than clicking each plate. The game has also had a pass to reduce unnecessary redraws on mobile devices, so the action stays responsive on smaller screens.
